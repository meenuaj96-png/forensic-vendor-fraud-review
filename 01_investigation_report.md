# Investigation Report: Meridian Components Pvt Ltd

Fictional case study | Draft for review | Period: 1 January to 31 December 2025

## 1. Executive summary
A hotline tip alleged that the Procurement Manager (E-014) was directing payments to connected vendors and arranging facilitation payments to customs officials. I reviewed 48 vendor payments (INR 1,16,85,178) and the vendor master.

Two vendors stand out. Sterling Marine Consultants received INR 49,84,000 (42.7% of spend) in ten payments, every one just under the INR 5,00,000 dual-approval limit, paid on Saturdays to a UAE account, for "port liaison consulting". Orion Clearance Associates received INR 14,50,000 in five round-sum "facilitation" payments. Both vendors were created in late December 2024 and approved by E-014 alone.

Taken together with a duplicate payment (INR 3,12,450) and an INR 1,85,000 hospitality payment linked to a customs visit, INR 69,31,450 (59.3% of spend) is flagged for follow-up. These are indicators, not findings of fraud. The evidence available does not show what the vendors actually did, who owns them, or where the money went.

## 2. Scope and approach
Reviewed: vendor payment ledger and vendor master for 2025, and the INR 5,00,000 dual-approval policy.
Not reviewed: invoices, contracts, emails, bank statements, or any interviews.

Approach: (1) profile spend by vendor; (2) run five rule-based tests in Excel (see workpaper); (3) compare flagged vendors against onboarding records; (4) assess anti-bribery exposure; (5) set out next steps.

## 5. Assessment
The combination of threshold avoidance, offshore payee, no identified deliverables and a single approver is consistent with a possible fictitious or related-party vendor. The Orion and hospitality payments raise bribery risk because customs officials are public officials. Neither conclusion can be drawn without further evidence.

Legal exposure is summarised in `03_fcpa_ukba_gap_review.md`. Note that the UK Bribery Act has no exception for facilitation payments.

## 6. Recommended next steps
1. Preserve evidence and place a hold on further payments to Sterling and Orion.
2. Obtain contracts, invoices, proof of services and approval records.
3. Establish beneficial ownership of both vendors from public registers, and check for links to E-014.
4. Take legal advice before interviewing E-014 or the approvers.
5. Review gifts and hospitality records involving public officials.
6. Recover the duplicate payment.
7. Assess reporting and disclosure obligations with counsel.

## 7. Control improvements
Split-payment alerts near approval limits; duplicate-invoice blocking; two-person vendor onboarding with KYC and adverse media checks; conflict-of-interest declarations with periodic vendor-employee matching; a written ban on facilitation payments and a register for hospitality involving officials; due diligence on all customs, port and liaison intermediaries.

## 8. Limitations
This review relied on a small fictional dataset and no interviews or source documents. Findings should be read as red flags warranting investigation.
